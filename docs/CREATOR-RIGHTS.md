# Creator rights and provenance

Creators retain ownership of submitted IP. Registration and creator intake do not transfer ownership. Registration stores the provider's representation; it is evidence of an affirmation, not independent legal verification. CrossingKey receives only the expressly represented distribution/commercialization permissions needed for the registered offer.

AI training defaults to false. Derivative permission defaults to false. No training pipeline, sublicense engine or transformation execution is added. Setting a permission field does not itself perform an activity. The operator must review the license, existing obligations and release authority before activating sales or publishing metadata.

Every capability retains its rights affirmation version and timestamp, versioned license text, provider, source provenance and optional content hash. Digital delivery requires verified SHA-256 bytes. Provenance supports a transformations array but no transformation entries are invented. Private filesystem locations appear only in operator configuration, never public capability metadata.

Revocation policy must explain how new sales stop and how existing licensed buyers are treated. Disable a capability or suspend a provider to stop new purchases. Existing entitlements, receipts and financial records are preserved for support, disputes and operator review. This build never deletes rights evidence automatically.

First-party seed inventory was imported as private planned records under the user's explicit engineering authorization. That does not publish it, enable sales, or override its single-buyer internal-commercial-use license and no-resale restrictions.
